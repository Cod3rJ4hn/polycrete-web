## Grammar: Split stage

Chosen because the business IS a comparison. A concreter who pours driveways
sells a new thing; Polycrete sells the difference between the slab you have and
the floor it becomes. That is two sides held in tension and resolved, which is
the definition of this grammar.

Why the other seven lost:

- **Filmic one-shot** — carries a burden of proof, and loses it here: there is
  no footage, no generation budget, and a single emotional arc undersells a
  page whose whole argument is a comparison.
- **Continuous world** — requires worldflight and generated video legs. No
  budget, no key, and a floor is not a journey through a place.
- **Chaptered editorial** — for long-form substance. A tradie quote page is not
  a read.
- **Live surface** — for software. There is no product surface to operate.
- **Typographic poster** — explicitly the right answer when there are no good
  assets. There are 16 good assets; this would waste them.
- **Gallery / catalog** — fits the four finishes, but the visitor's real
  question here is "should I believe this is worth it", not "what are the
  options". Gallery forbids persuasion in labels, and persuasion is the job.
- **Rhythmic cutlist** — for energy brands. Wrong register for a trade whose
  pitch is care and patience.

**Grammar constraints honoured:** no fixed bar (the divider is the chrome); no
full-bleed anything before the resolve; no centred copy; no corner-anchored
hero; both columns carry real content throughout; the close is the collapse.
**Bans observed:** no `pan`, no `spotlight`, no `magnet`, no `scrub`, no `drift`
— two grounds, one per side, and they hold.

## Nav treatment

The divider is the chrome. It runs the full height of the viewport, fixed,
carrying: the label for each side, the progress of the argument, the grit
readout during the peak, and a permanently tappable phone number. A trade site
that buries the phone is broken, and this grammar forbids a bar, so the phone
lives in the divider. On a phone the divider rotates to a horizontal rule and
keeps the same job.

## Signature move: the pass ladder

Polished concrete is made by grinding the same slab repeatedly at successively
finer grits. The page does that literally. Through the peak act, scroll drives a
single `--polish` value from 0 to 1; the grit readout in the divider climbs the
ladder; and the floor on the right is re-finished live off that one value —
reflection opacity, specular sharpness, contrast and saturation all move
together, with a grinder-width band travelling down the slab as the boundary
between done and not done. One input, the whole surface changes.

Not a wipe between two photographs. The finished state is computed, so the
visitor can stop halfway and hold a half-polished floor.

## Act-sequence shape

Six acts, 11.4vh total — deliberately outside the 13.6–13.8vh / 6–7 act band
that the skill flags as a prior fingerprint. One act at 4.0vh carries the peak,
more than double the next largest.

## Close pattern

The collapse. The divider travels to the left edge, the finished side takes the
full width, and the phone number resolves as the only thing left on screen.

## The feeling curve

| Act | Feeling | What on screen causes it |
|---|---|---|
| 1 The split | Recognition | Their own dull slab on the left, a mirror floor on the right, both at once |
| 2 What it costs | Resignation | Plain type, no photography, the cheapest-looking act on the page |
| 3 The passes | **Satisfaction (PEAK)** | The floor polishing itself under their scroll, grit climbing as they go |
| 4 The finishes | Confidence | Four real finishes, four real photographs, no sales language |
| 5 Who's had it done | Trust | Four real names and four real reviews, counted up from zero |
| 6 Call Corey | Resolve | The split collapses, one number fills the screen |

No two adjacent acts share a feeling. Act 2 is deliberately the quietest on the
page, because it sits directly before the peak.

## The peak

**"You scroll and the concrete polishes itself."**

Lives in act 3. Gets 4.0vh, the only generated-free hero moment on the page, and
the silence of act 2 immediately before it.

## The tell-someone sentence

It's the site where **you scroll and the floor polishes itself under your hand.**

## Authored silence

Act 2 has no photography at all, on purpose. If the verification pass reports it
as visually dead, that is the intent, not a defect — it is the flat beat that
makes the peak land.
